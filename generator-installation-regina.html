What's Included in a Generator Installation

Installing a standby generator involves significant site preparation, electrical work, and compliance with local building codes. Here's what we handle:

  • Site preparation — packing and levelling soil, concrete pad installation
  • Generator placement and landing
  • Automatic transfer switch installation — detects SaskPower voltage drop, starts generator automatically
  • Natural gas or diesel fuel line hookup
  • All TSASK permits and inspections
  • Full commissioning and test run

Natural Gas vs. Diesel — Which Is Right for You?

Both fuel types have their advantages depending on your location and load requirements.

  • Natural gas — convenient city connection, cleaner burning, lower maintenance, quieter operation
  • Diesel — higher power output, on-site fuel storage for rural or farm use, more durable for heavy loads

How Much Power Do You Need?

Power requirements are measured in kilowatts. We calculate the total wattage of all systems you need to run during an outage — heat, refrigeration, sump pumps, livestock systems, and grain handling for farms — then size the generator accordingly. An oversized or undersized generator is a waste of money.

  • What does the warranty cover? — Generac and Cummins warranties vary by model — we'll walk you through coverage before purchase.
